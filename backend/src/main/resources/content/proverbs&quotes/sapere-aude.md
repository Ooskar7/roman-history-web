# Sapere Aude

## English translation

Dare to be wise.

## Author and attribution

Horace

## Meaning

Have the courage to begin cultivating judgment. Wisdom requires action now, rather than endless preparation or postponement.

## Cultural context

In Epistles 1.2, Horace addresses Lollius and draws ethical lessons from Homer. He urges his reader to start living wisely instead of waiting for the perfect moment.

## Significance and legacy

The phrase later became closely associated with the Enlightenment through Immanuel Kant’s 1784 essay, which used it to urge independent understanding. That later use develops the ancient line in a new setting.

## A note on the wording

“Dare to know” is another common translation; in Horace the focus is practical wisdom and beginning to act on it.
