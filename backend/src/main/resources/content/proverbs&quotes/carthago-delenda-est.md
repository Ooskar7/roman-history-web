# Carthago Delenda Est

## English translation

Carthage must be destroyed.

## Author and attribution

Traditionally associated with Cato the Elder

## Meaning

A forceful demand that a perceived threat be eliminated. Delenda expresses obligation, not merely a prediction that destruction will happen.

## Cultural context

Plutarch describes Cato repeatedly urging the destruction of Carthage in Senate debates. Rome’s old rival had recovered economically after the Second Punic War, and Cato pressed for renewed confrontation.

## Significance and legacy

Cato’s campaign helped promote war. The Third Punic War began in 149 BCE; Rome destroyed Carthage in 146 BCE, after Cato’s death. The saying now also describes an insistently repeated political demand.

## A note on the wording

This familiar Latin slogan summarizes the ancient tradition; it is not a securely preserved verbatim transcript of Cato’s speeches.
