# Non scholae sed vitae discimus

## English translation

We learn not for school, but for life.

## Author and attribution

Later adaptation of Seneca the Younger

## Meaning

Education should prepare us to live well, not merely to satisfy classroom requirements. It values understanding that remains useful beyond formal study.

## Cultural context

At the close of Letter 106, Seneca criticizes overly subtle intellectual exercises. His actual sentence says the reverse: people learn for school instead of life.

## Significance and legacy

The inversion transforms a complaint about misplaced priorities into a positive educational ideal. It is a useful example of how a later motto can preserve a writer’s concern while changing his words.

## A note on the wording

Seneca wrote “non vitae sed scholae discimus.” The familiar motto should therefore be labeled an adaptation, not an exact quotation.
