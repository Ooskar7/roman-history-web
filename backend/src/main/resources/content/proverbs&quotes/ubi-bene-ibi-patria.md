# Ubi bene, ibi patria

## English translation

Where life is good, there is my homeland.

## Author and attribution

Proverbial form; related verse quoted by Cicero

## Meaning

Belonging can depend on where one can live well rather than only on birthplace. It can suggest adaptability, but also raise questions about loyalty and comfort.

## Cultural context

In Tusculan Disputations 5.108, Cicero discusses exile and quotes a verse from Pacuvius’s Teucer expressing that one’s homeland is wherever things are well.

## Significance and legacy

The thought offers a way to reflect on displacement and attachment to place. It also exposes a tension between personal well-being and the Roman ideal of duty to one’s civic community.

## A note on the wording

The quoted ancient wording is “patria est, ubicumque est bene.” The familiar ubi bene form is a proverbial reshaping, not that exact verse.
