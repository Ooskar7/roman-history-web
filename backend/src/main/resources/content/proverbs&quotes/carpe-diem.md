# Carpe Diem

## English translation

Seize the day.

## Author and attribution

Horace

## Meaning

Make use of the present rather than relying on an unknowable future. Carpe also suggests plucking or gathering something ripe, giving the advice a gentler sense than reckless urgency.

## Cultural context

In Odes 1.11, Horace addresses Leuconoe, discourages attempts to calculate the future, and urges her to limit distant hopes. The poem belongs to the literary world of Augustan Rome.

## Significance and legacy

The expression became a central theme in later poetry about time, pleasure, and mortality. Its popularity often obscures the original emphasis on measured enjoyment and uncertain expectations.

## A note on the wording

The familiar English translation is idiomatic; “pluck the day” preserves the verb’s image.
