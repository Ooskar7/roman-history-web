# O Tempora, O Mores!

## English translation

Oh, the times! Oh, the morals!

## Author and attribution

Marcus Tullius Cicero

## Meaning

An exclamation of outrage at the behavior of one’s own age. Mores refers to customs or moral habits, not simply manners at the dinner table.

## Cultural context

In his First Catilinarian speech of 63 BCE, Cicero denounces Catiline and expresses indignation that the Senate knows of the danger yet allows him to remain present.

## Significance and legacy

The phrase became shorthand for complaints about social decline. Its rhetorical power shows how Roman politicians used moral judgment to intensify political accusations.

## A note on the wording

Cicero’s speech is a partisan intervention in a crisis, so its portrait of universal moral collapse should be read as rhetoric, not neutral social description.
