# Alea Iacta Est

## English translation

The die is cast.

## Author and attribution

Attributed to Julius Caesar

## Meaning

A decisive step has been taken and its consequences must now unfold. The image is a gaming die already thrown: the risk has been accepted, but the outcome is uncertain.

## Cultural context

Ancient biographies associate the saying with Caesar’s crossing of the Rubicon with troops in 49 BCE. Entering Italy under arms challenged senatorial authority and opened civil war against Pompey and his allies.

## Significance and legacy

The crossing became a symbol of an irreversible commitment. The ensuing wars contributed to the end of the Republic; the quotation commemorates the choice rather than causing those events.

## A note on the wording

Suetonius gives the Latin in the order iacta alea est. Plutarch reports a Greek expression closer to “Let the die be cast.” These are later literary accounts, not a verbatim contemporary record.
