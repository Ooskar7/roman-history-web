# Panem et circenses

## English translation

Bread and circuses.

## Author and attribution

Juvenal

## Meaning

Food and entertainment can absorb public attention while political participation declines. Circenses refers to circus games, especially racing, rather than a modern traveling circus.

## Cultural context

In Satire 10, Juvenal contrasts the Roman people’s former political power with what he presents as their reduced interests under imperial rule.

## Significance and legacy

The expression remains a criticism of distraction and political complacency. It should not replace a fuller account of Roman food supply, public entertainment, or ordinary people’s political concerns.

## A note on the wording

This is a satirist’s sharp judgment, not a neutral survey proving that all Romans cared only about games and grain.
